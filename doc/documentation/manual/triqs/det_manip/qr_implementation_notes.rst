How does ``det_manip_qr`` work ?
################################

``det_manip_qr`` is a numerically more robust alternative to ``det_manip``. Instead of tracking the
inverse matrix with the Sherman-Morrison-Woodbury formulas (which keep no pivoting and can drift), it
tracks an orthogonal **QR factorization** of the matrix and updates it incrementally with Givens
rotations, following the technique of the w2dynamics CT-QMC solver. Orthogonal transformations have a
bounded growth factor, so the determinant is backward stable and a singular configuration is detected
cleanly (an exact zero on the diagonal of :math:`R`).

These notes use the **same names for the mathematical symbols as for the variables in the code**
(``c++/triqs/det_manip/det_manip_qr.hpp`` and ``c++/triqs/det_manip/lapack_qr.hpp``).

Representation
==============

The factorization is kept in **internal (append) order** and mapped back to the logical order of
:math:`A` by a row and a column permutation. With :math:`B = Q R` the internal-order matrix,

.. math:: B_{a t} = A_{\,\mathrm{row\_perm}[a],\ \mathrm{col\_perm}[t]}, \qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad A = P_r\, B\, P_c^{T},

where :math:`P_r` and :math:`P_c` are the permutation matrices :math:`P_r e_a = e_{\mathrm{row\_perm}[a]}`
and :math:`P_c e_t = e_{\mathrm{col\_perm}[t]}`. A full factorization produces
:math:`\mathrm{row\_perm}=\mathrm{id}` and :math:`\mathrm{col\_perm}` equal to the pivot; only the
incremental insert/remove make :math:`\mathrm{row\_perm}` non-trivial.

Two consequences are used everywhere. The **determinant** (``determinant()``) is

.. math:: \det A = \mathrm{sign}\cdot \mathrm{det\_q}\cdot \prod_{i} R_{ii},
          \qquad \mathrm{sign}=\operatorname{sgn}(\mathrm{row\_perm})\,\operatorname{sgn}(\mathrm{col\_perm}),

and the **inverse** (``compute_inverse``), from :math:`A^{-1}=P_c\, R^{-1} Q^{H}\, P_r^{T}`, is filled
element-wise as

.. math:: \bigl(A^{-1}\bigr)_{\,\mathrm{col\_perm}[t],\ \mathrm{row\_perm}[a]} = \bigl(R^{-1}Q^{H}\bigr)_{t a},

where :math:`R^{-1}Q^{H}` is obtained by the triangular solve :math:`R X = Q^{H}`
(``nda::lapack::trtrs``).


Full factorization
==================

``factorize`` computes a column-pivoted QR :math:`A\,\Pi = Q R` with ``nda::lapack::geqp3`` (then
``orgqr``/``ungqr`` form :math:`Q` explicitly). The determinant of :math:`Q` is the product of the
Householder reflector determinants

.. math:: \mathrm{det\_q} = \prod_i d(\tau_i), \qquad
          d(\tau) = \begin{cases} 1 & \tau = 0 \\ -1 & \tau\ \text{real} \\ -\,(\tau/|\tau|)^2 & \tau\ \text{complex} \end{cases}

(``householder_det_factor``). On exit :math:`\mathrm{row\_perm}=\mathrm{id}`,
:math:`\mathrm{col\_perm}=\Pi`, and :math:`\mathrm{sign}=\operatorname{sgn}(\Pi)`. This is also the
*safety-net* path: it is re-run periodically, and as soon as the cheap condition estimate
:math:`\lvert R_{00}/R_{N-1,N-1}\rvert` (``condition_estimate``) exceeds a threshold, to
re-orthogonalize :math:`Q`, re-pivot, and reset the internal order.


Givens rotations
================

A plane rotation is the unitary :math:`2\times2` matrix
:math:`G=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} c & s\\ -\overline{s} & c\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)` with :math:`c`
real; ``make_givens`` (LAPACK ``lartg``) returns the :math:`G` that maps
:math:`G\,(f,\,g)^{T}=(r,\,0)^{T}`. Four primitives apply it, paired so that the product
:math:`Q M` is invariant, :math:`(Q\,G^{H})(G\,M)=Q M`:

* ``apply_rows`` / ``apply_cols`` apply :math:`G` to two rows of :math:`M` / two columns of :math:`Q`;
* ``apply_rows_adjoint`` / ``apply_cols_adjoint`` apply :math:`G^{H}`.


Addition of a line and a column, or more (grow)
===============================================

To insert :math:`k` rows and :math:`k` columns (``grow_insert`` for :math:`k=1`, ``grow_k`` for any
:math:`k`; wired to ``try_insert``/``try_insert2``/``try_insert_k``), the new rows are appended at the
internal front and the new columns at the :math:`R`-end. With ``rows`` the :math:`k\times n` block of
new-row entries (in :math:`R`-column order), ``cols`` the :math:`n\times k` block of new-column entries
(in internal-row order) and ``dot`` the :math:`k\times k` corner, the bordered matrix

.. math:: M = \begin{pmatrix} \mathrm{rows} & \mathrm{dot} \\ R & Q^{H}\,\mathrm{cols} \end{pmatrix}

is :math:`k`-Hessenberg (zeros below the :math:`k`-th subdiagonal). ``qr_k_hessenberg`` reduces it to
the new upper-triangular :math:`R` with a sequence of Givens rotations, which ``rotate_q`` applies (as
:math:`G^{H}`) to :math:`Q_0=\operatorname{blockdiag}(I_k, Q)` to give the new :math:`Q`. The reflectors
and the unit block have determinant :math:`1`, so :math:`\mathrm{det\_q}` is unchanged and
:math:`\det A' = \mathrm{sign}'\cdot\mathrm{det\_q}\cdot\prod_i R'_{ii}`. ``row_perm`` / ``col_perm``
receive the new logical positions (existing indices shifted by the multi-insertion offset map) and
:math:`\mathrm{sign}'` is recomputed with ``permutation_sign``.

(The :math:`k=1` ``grow_insert`` instead appends the single new row/column at the internal *end* and
clears the resulting bottom row with one Givens sweep — the same update specialized for speed.)


Removal of a line and a column, or more (shrink)
================================================

To remove the internal row :math:`a^{*}` (with :math:`\mathrm{row\_perm}[a^{*}]=i`) and the
:math:`R`-column :math:`t^{*}` (with :math:`\mathrm{col\_perm}[t^{*}]=j`), ``shrink_one`` proceeds in two
phases (``shrink_chain`` chains it in descending order for :math:`k>1`, wired to
``try_remove``/``try_remove2``/``try_remove_k``):

#. Reduce row :math:`a^{*}` of :math:`Q` to a single nonzero :math:`\delta` in column :math:`0` with
   column Givens rotations (``apply_cols`` on :math:`Q`, ``apply_rows_adjoint`` on :math:`R`, which
   makes :math:`R` upper-Hessenberg). Because :math:`Q` is unitary, :math:`|\delta|=1` and column
   :math:`0` of the reduced :math:`Q` equals :math:`\delta\, e_{a^{*}}`; dropping row :math:`a^{*}`,
   column :math:`0` of :math:`Q` and row :math:`0` of :math:`R` yields the factorization of the
   row-removed matrix.

#. Drop :math:`R`-column :math:`t^{*}` and retriangularize the resulting upper-Hessenberg :math:`R`
   with row Givens (applied as :math:`G^{H}` to the columns of :math:`Q`).

Expanding :math:`\det Q` along the reduced row gives the determinant-of-:math:`Q` update

.. math:: \mathrm{det\_q}' = \mathrm{det\_q}\cdot \overline{\delta}\cdot (-1)^{a^{*}} ,

and :math:`\det A'` follows from :math:`\mathrm{sign}'\cdot\mathrm{det\_q}'\cdot\prod_i R'_{ii}` with
the shrunk ``row_perm`` / ``col_perm`` (the removed entry dropped, larger logical indices decremented).


Swap and roll
=============

``swap_row`` / ``swap_col`` exchange two logical rows / columns. They act in the permutation layer
only: swap the two ``x_values`` (resp. ``y_values``), the two rows (cols) of ``mat`` and the matching
``row_perm`` (``col_perm``) entries, and flip :math:`\mathrm{sign}` and :math:`\det A` — :math:`Q` and
:math:`R` are untouched (:math:`O(N)`, no Givens). ``roll_matrix`` cyclically shifts the values and
re-runs ``factorize`` to resynchronize the factorization.


Self-check
==========

``verify_qr`` is a debug routine asserting the invariants of the cached factorization to a tolerance:
:math:`Q^{H}Q=I`, :math:`R` upper-triangular, and the reconstruction
:math:`A_{\,\mathrm{row\_perm}[a],\ \mathrm{col\_perm}[t]} = (Q R)_{a t}`.
